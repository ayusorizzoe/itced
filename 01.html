Sanct

discursive product research conceptual graphic

Restraint has always been a product. Sanct just made the packaging honest.


Sanct is a speculative product catalog and printed publication for a fictional wellness brand that sells bodily restraint as self-care. Seven products. Full luxury format, editorial layout, marble surfaces, restrained typography. It looks like something you'd find on a bathroom shelf in a hotel that charges $900 a night. It operates like something else entirely.


See Full Catalog ↗

The catalog is fictional. The logic it runs on isn't. Wellness culture and religious doctrine share the same core proposition: the body is a problem that requires management, and managing it correctly is a form of virtue. A smart ring tracking your sleep and a confessor tracking your sins are not structurally different. Both promise control. Both monetize the anxiety of failing to achieve it. Both require you to believe, first, that your body is something to be corrected.

Sanct sits in the name itself, somewhere between sanctified and sanitized. The project asks whether the wellness consumer and the devoted penitent are, at this point, performing the same ritual under different art direction. The answer it arrives at is yes. Neither the desire nor the hypocrisy disappears. They just get a rebrand every decade or so.

Sanct doesn't moralize about any of this. It sells to it.

The project pulls on the Puritan roots of American attitudes toward sex and the body, a tradition in which physical desire was coded as moral failure and discipline as proximity to God. That framework didn't dissolve. It moved indoors. It settled into domestic structures, institutional religion, conservative social policy, producing generations of people who learned early that desire was something to be managed, hidden, and ashamed of. What makes the current moment particularly strange is that this internalized shame coexists with a public culture that has never been more willing to use sex to sell things. The body is simultaneously the most monetized image in commercial culture and the most policed reality in private life. The gap between what is permitted on a billboard and what is permitted in a bedroom is enormous, and largely unacknowledged. That gap is where Sanct operates.

Graphically, the catalog adopts the full aesthetic vocabulary of high-end wellness, white packaging, clean sans-serif, restrained layout, and cross-contaminates it with ecclesiastical typography, gold-foil detailing, and medieval woodcut illustration. Blackletter type sits next to product weight measurements. A horned figure dances in foil on a pillar candle. Every graphic decision is a deliberate collision between two systems of belief that share more infrastructure than either would publicly admit. The luxury wellness brand and the religious institution. Both selling discipline as devotion. Both building revenue on the body's complicated relationship with its own desires.

Reading through the product copy, the language of sealing, adhesion, restraint framed as care, it became clear that the logic of chastity and the logic of kink are operating closer to each other than either tradition would want to acknowledge. Both are organized around control, ritual, consent, and the body as a site of negotiated power. The catalog can be read as devotional or erotic without changing a single word. That's not a coincidence. That's the point.
