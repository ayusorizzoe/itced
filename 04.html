without kingdom

product conceptual experimental

A jewelry collection built entirely from flat brass stock, by hand.


Without Kingdom is a collection of three brass jewelry pieces, a necklace, a pin, and a ring, handcrafted entirely from brass sheet using no casting. The pieces draw loosely from the visual language of medieval metalwork, shields, insignia, and figural ornament. The collection is the material record of a process: learning to make jewelry by hand, under a single constraint, from one material only.


The project was built around a total material constraint: brass sheet only, no lost wax casting, no prefabricated elements. Every form had to be derived from flat stock. That constraint was not conceptual. It was structural, and it determined what techniques needed to be learned and in what order.

The ring is a box construction, its band and face shaped from separate pieces of sheet, fitted and soldered into a single solid object. The pin layers multiple brass elements, held together through riveting that allows each part to sit independently while reading as a whole. The necklace is the most technically complex piece: the wolf figure is cut, shaped, textured, and assembled across several connected parts, with rivets at each leg joint allowing the limbs to move freely, like a puppet, when worn. The black patina was tested and controlled rather than applied uniformly, used to deepen recessed surfaces and produce the sense of age and oxidation.

The techniques encountered across the collection, sawing, forming, soldering, riveting, hammering, and patination, were each learned in direct response to what each piece demanded of the material. The objects look old. They were made yesterday.
