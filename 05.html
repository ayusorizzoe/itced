Runa

product experimental graphic

A speculative collectible toy built around what open source design and accessible fabrication could mean for the people making them.


Runa is a speculative collectible toy designed entirely in Blender and produced through FDM 3D printing. The character, the clothing system, the packaging, and the modular assembly logic were developed as a single object to think with, not a solution, but a provocation.

The starting point was the street toy markets in Mexico City, where merchants sell mass-produced 3D printed figures they didn't design, using technology they increasingly have access to. Blender is free. FDM printers are getting cheaper. Kids are already sending their Roblox characters out to be printed by someone else and paying for it. The tools exist. The project is a speculation around what it looks like when the people selling these things start making them too, and what open source design and accessible fabrication could mean for that.

The clothing pieces are separate FDM prints that attach and detach from the base figure, each split to feed onto the limbs without hardware. The system is designed around what a single-color printer can do today and what a multi-color machine could do soon. The renders show the speculative version. The prototype shows where the technology actually is.
