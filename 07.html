Hangout

product research interactive

A prototype for a problem the market identified but hasn't solved.


Hangout is a mobile app prototype for making plans with friends and meeting new people around you based on shared interests and location. The project started in 2021 and was updated and polished in the summer of 2023. It never shipped.

The observation behind it was simple: social media was supposed to make connection easier, and in some ways it did, but it also produced a specific kind of loneliness where you text people every day and rarely see them in person. The question Hangout started from was what an app that closed that gap could actually look like.

The answer it proposed was a complete social layer: a real-time map showing where friends are, a mood and status system, interest-based friend discovery, event creation with privacy controls, a shared activity feed, and a memories calendar tied to past events. Not one of those features. All of them, integrated into a single product logic built around getting people to meet in person.

By the time the prototype was finished, apps like BeReal, Partiful, and Amigos existed and were gaining traction. All three were founded around the same time. None of them were solving the same problem. Partiful is event planning. BeReal is anti-performative photo sharing. Amigos is the closest, connecting people nearby through shared interests, and it has 800,000 users across seven countries and still hasn't broken through. The problem Hangout was pointing at remains largely unsolved. The app didn't ship. The problem didn't go away.
